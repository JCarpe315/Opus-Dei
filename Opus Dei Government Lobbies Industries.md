# Opus Dei: Members in Government, Lobbies, Industries, and Power Structures

## What This Document Is

This document is a deep-dive research compilation focused specifically on one question: **Where are Opus Dei members positioned in the structures of power?** This includes government at all levels, lobbying organizations, special interest groups, intelligence agencies, judicial systems, think tanks, media, education, and major industries — banking, energy, technology, defense, pharmaceuticals, real estate, and more.

What follows is not an opinion piece. It is a compilation of documented facts, allegations, and official statements. Every named individual is sourced from publicly available records: Wikipedia's list of prominent members, investigative journalism, court documents, official biographies, and organizational disclosures. Where a connection is alleged rather than proven, it is labeled as such. Where no documented connection exists, that is stated explicitly.

The goal is completeness. If a name, position, or sector has been documented in connection with Opus Dei, it is here.

## Part I: The Strategy — Why This Matters

### The "Intellectuals"

Josemaría Escrivá, Opus Dei's founder, explicitly targeted what the organization calls "the intellectuals" — people in positions of power who could influence society from the top down. This was not incidental; it was the core strategy from the organization's earliest days. As journalist Gareth Gore documents in his book *Opus*, Escrivá believed that converting a single judge, banker, or politician was worth more than converting a thousand ordinary faithful.

The strategy is simple: identify people with authority, befriend them, offer spiritual guidance, and gradually integrate them into Opus Dei's worldview. The endgame, as Gore summarizes it, is "the complete 're-Christianization' of society — of everything from education to politics, the courts and the private life of each and every citizen."

### The Washington Penetration

The largest Opus Dei community in the United States is not in Catholic-heavy Boston, Chicago, or New York — it is in the Washington, D.C. area. Officially, Opus Dei claims 3,000 members in the US; Gore was told by sources that approximately 800 are in the Washington metropolitan area.

The Catholic Information Center (CIC) sits at 1501 K Street NW — two blocks from the White House, in the heart of the lobbying district. It bills itself as "the closest tabernacle to the White House." Under Father C. John McCloskey (1998–2002), it became a central meeting place for conservative Catholics in power. Slate called McCloskey "the Catholic Church's K Street lobbyist." The Catholic Herald called him the "unofficial chaplain to the upper echelons of the Republican Party" who ran his converting operation "like the brokerage business."

### The "Troika" Model

A powerful network operates at the intersection of three institutions:

1. **The Federalist Society** — shapes conservative judicial philosophy and pipelines judges
2. **The Catholic Information Center (CIC)** — provides spiritual infrastructure and elite networking for conservative Catholics in Washington
3. **Opus Dei** — provides the ideological framework, member discipline, and global coordination

These three institutions are not formally merged, but their leadership overlaps extensively, and they reinforce each other's influence.

## Part III: Intelligence Agencies & Law Enforcement

### Documented Connections

**Robert Hanssen (FBI)**
- Supernumerary member of Opus Dei
- FBI agent who spied for Soviet Union and Russia
- Described as "possibly the worst intelligence disaster in US history"
- Began spying 1979; stopped briefly after confessing to an Opus Dei priest; resumed 1985; arrested 2001
- The critical question: Did Opus Dei know? Hanssen confessed his espionage to an Opus Dei priest at some point. Journalist Gareth Gore alleges Opus Dei knew and kept quiet to protect Washington recruitment — but no independent confirmation exists beyond Gore's claim.

**Chile — The Allende Coup and Opus Dei**

- Members included lawyers, free-market economists, and executives from influential publications
- Hernan Cubillos (founder of *Que Pasa*, an Opus Dei magazine, and publisher of *El Mercurio*) was a leading IGS staffer
- *El Mercurio* received at least $1.5 million from the CIA
- After the 1973 coup, IGS technocrats became cabinet members and advisors to the Pinochet junta
- Cubillos served as foreign minister under Pinochet (1978–1980)
- The Monday Club — a group of Chilean business leaders, newspaper owners, and right-wing politicians — met every Monday at Cubillos' office on Lord Cochrane Street in Santiago
- US Ambassador Edward Korry assembled business leaders and told them "not a nut or bolt will be allowed to reach Chile under Allende"
